# Decisions

Decisions taken while building the site autonomously, with the reasoning. Newest at the bottom of each section.

## Originals, links and licenses

- **Korostoff's original URL is offline.** `https://mkorostoff.github.io/1-pixel-wealth/` returned HTTP 404 on 2026-09-26 (checked via two independent fetch paths); `github.com/MKorostoff/1-pixel-wealth` also returned 404/403. The site is still credited with its original URL (marked "offline when checked"), plus the working git mirror at hacktivis.me and the original repository URL. The mirror's file list contains no LICENSE, so the code is treated as all-rights-reserved and nothing was reused; the site is a from-scratch reimplementation of the idea (1 px = 1,000 $, horizontal scroll, comparison markers).
- **Humphrey Yang's original**: the rice videos are on TikTok (`@humphreytalks`, videos 6798276393634467077 "Rice. Part 2: Jeff Bezos net worth" and the follow-up). TikTok returns a shell page to robots, so the link was verified through the BuzzFeed News and Forbes coverage from February/March 2020 which name the handle and the scale (1 grain = 100,000 $). Video content is not reused; only the scale.
- **Neal Agarwal's "Spend Bill Gates' Money"** has no public source or license; only the format is reused. The page itself is reachable (bot-protected for scripts, fine in browsers).
- **Other originals**: searched for earlier "spend the money" and rice visualisations. Found only clones of neal.fun and post-2020 repetitions of Yang's idea; nothing earlier to credit. Noted on the Credits page.

## Data sources and conflict rules

- **Primary wealth source: Forbes Real-Time Billionaires** (public JSON endpoint, reachable, daily). Chosen over the annual list because it is the most recent reputable estimate. The annual list is attached as an alternative source per person. Bloomberg's index is blocked for robots and therefore not automated; Manager Magazin is paywalled, so its October 2025 list is entered by hand from the Wikipedia snapshot (`data/overrides/wealth_alt_sources.json`) with the Wikipedia URL as `source_url`.
- **Conflict rule**: the headline figure is the most recent estimate from a reputable list (Forbes RT). Older or family-pooled figures (Forbes annual, Manager Magazin) are shown as the range in the tooltip, never averaged. Where Manager Magazin pools siblings (Klatten + Quandt, Albrecht jr. + Heister), the joint figure is attached to both persons with a note.
- **German top ten = Forbes citizenship field "Germany"**. Consequence: Klaus-Michael Kühne (listed by Forbes' annual list at 41.9 bn $, absent from the real-time list) and the Merck and Reimann families (not itemised by Forbes) are missing from the top ten. They are listed separately on the Germany page from Manager Magazin, so readers see the gap rather than a silently different list.
- **Family entries**: flagged when the source says "& family"/"Familie". A per-person value is shown only when a source states the number of named persons; otherwise the UI says "no reliable data". Karl Albrecht Jr. and Beate Heister are listed individually by Forbes, so they are marked as one named person each.
- **Reference values**: Destatis (median full-time gross earnings 2025, monthly April 2025), Bundesbank PHF 2023 (median and mean household net wealth, shares, Gini), Census (US median household income 2025), Fed SCF 2022 (US median family net worth), Georgetown CEW 2021 (US lifetime earnings). The German full-time median and the US household median are different concepts; both are labelled, and the methodology says so.
- **Lifetime earnings Germany**: no official single number exists. The site uses a transparent calculation (median × 45 years) labelled as derived, with the IAB-Kurzbericht 18/2022 range (1.10–2.49 M € for people with vocational training) in the definition. Rejected: picking a midpoint of the IAB range (that would be an own estimate).
- **Total German household net wealth** (needed for the bottom-50 % comparison) is derived as PHF mean × Mikrozensus household count, labelled derived and estimate, with the caveat that surveys under-cover the top.
- **Rice grain weight**: 21.65 mg per grain from the 1000-grain weight of milled rice of cultivar M-210 (US patent, a primary laboratory value). The IRRI fact sheets describe the measurement but publish no single number; Yang's own weighing implies 21.6 mg, so the value is consistent with the original. Bowl (200 g), sack (25 kg) and truck (25 t) are declared unit definitions, not empirical claims.
- **Prices**: only items with a verifiable source were kept. Dropped candidates without a parseable source page: car list prices (Volkswagen, Porsche pages are JS-rendered), Bloomberg values, Big Mac index (GitHub blocked in the build sandbox). Derived prices (ICE 4 = 6 bn € / 137, social flat = 60 m² × 4,900 €/m², wind turbine = 5 MW × midpoint of 910–1,420 €/kW, Kita place = spending / children) show their formula in the tooltip and are marked as estimates.
- **BMW dividend**: the objections text originally cited an absolute payout to the Quandt/Klatten siblings; no primary source states that number, so the text now cites the sourced dividend per share (4.40 €, FY 2025) and the fact that they are the main beneficiaries.
- **Effective tax rates**: each study is tagged with its denominator ((a) taxable income, (b) economic income, (c) wealth) and whether corporate taxes are attributed. Saez/Zucman's 23 % vs. 24.2 % is included with the note that it is contested (Tax Policy Center, Auten/Splinter) because leaving it out would hide the most-cited figure; the objections chapter carries the criticism. The "German holding owner, profits retained: 0 % personal tax" row is a statement of the law (§ 32d EStG, § 8b KStG), not a study, and is labelled as such.
- **Norway**: the "they will leave" objection uses the Finance Ministry figure (82 leavers 2022–23) as reported by press, because the primary Reuters article is paywalled; the source entry says so.

## Pipeline

- **Plausibility threshold 40 %** (configurable per run). A fetched value outside the band is not written; the value and reason go to `.pipeline/issue.md`, which the workflow turns into a single monthly issue (created or updated, label `data-review`). Last known good is always kept, and the site builds from the committed files, so a broken source can never blank the site.
- **Staleness**: each manual value carries `max_age_months` (or the file default). Stale entries go into the same issue with a `refresh_hint`. Age is measured from the reference period (`as_of`), not the retrieval date, which is why annual statistics get 15–36 months depending on their publication lag. The US SCF 2022 value is intentionally over its limit (the 2025 wave is expected late 2026): the first pipeline run demonstrates the stale path.
- **BLS**: the unregistered API allows 25 requests per day per IP; an optional `BLS_API_KEY` secret raises the limit. October 2025 CPI was never published (funding lapse), so annual averages tolerate one missing month and say so in the definition.
- **Eurostat instead of Destatis GENESIS** for the German price index: GENESIS needs an account; Eurostat's HICP for Germany is public and stable. Destatis figures for incomes are entered manually with the press-release URL and a refresh hint.
- **Changelog**: every accepted change is appended to `DATA-CHANGELOG.md` (same-day runs are merged into one section). Held-back values are not in the changelog; they are in the issue.
- **Link check** (`scripts/check-links.ts`) is informational: many publishers answer 403 to robots (Forbes, OECD, Tesla) while working in browsers. Results are attached to the issue as a collapsible table.

## Frontend

- **Home page redesign (second iteration, after user feedback)**: back to the original's block logic. Scale is an *area* scale: 1 px² = 1,000, so a million is a 32×32 block, a billion a 1000×1000 block, and the strip's bar height × width encodes the money (on narrow screens blocks become rectangles of the same area). Only the largest German fortune and the largest fortune on Earth are drawn, in that order, so the jump in scale is felt. The strip is a genuine horizontal scroll container (an empty spacer as wide as the strip, a canvas stuck to its left edge, redrawn from scrollLeft): on phones you swipe along it, on desktop a vertical mouse wheel over the strip moves it sideways only while it can still move, so at either end the wheel scrolls the page as usual; arrow keys and the scrollbar work natively. A first version drove the strip from the vertical scroll position (sticky stage in a tall wrapper); that felt wrong on touch devices, where a downward swipe produced sideways motion, and was replaced. No auto-scroll, no jump list, no scale switch. Rice and spend simulator are embedded below the strip; their standalone pages remain. Lottery comparisons (Eurojackpot cap, Powerball record) were added as sourced markers.

- **Vite + vanilla TypeScript** (no framework): the core is a canvas renderer with a small DOM overlay; a framework would add weight without helping. Build target ES2022 (native class fields; the ES2020 lowering produced a helper-ordering bug in the minified scroll chunk).
- **Virtual scrolling**: the strip is laid out in pure numbers (`src/scroll/engine.ts`); only bars intersecting the viewport are drawn on a viewport-sized canvas; labels and cards are absolutely positioned DOM elements created on entry and removed on exit. Total width at 1 px = 1,000 is about 3.3 billion pixels; nothing in the DOM is wider than the viewport.
- **"Money scrolled"** counts bar values left of the viewport edge (partial bars pro rata), not pixels, because bars are separated by gaps.
- **Objection cards** sit at fixed pixel offsets from the start of chosen bars (not fractions: 8 % of Musk's bar would be 65 million pixels away) so they appear shortly after a jump.
- **Routing**: `/<lang>/<page>/` with static shells per page, `?lang=` accepted and redirected to the path form, browser-language detection only when the URL has no language, last choice remembered in localStorage. Currency choice is `?cur=` + localStorage.
- **Scale**: 1 px = 1,000 in the display currency (default), 100 and 10,000 selectable, kept in `?scale=`. Deep links: `#x=<px>` (position) and `#at=<segmentId>`.
- **Accessibility**: canvas is `aria-hidden`; the stage is a focusable region with keyboard control; a polite live region announces the current bar and the counter; jump marks are buttons; source popovers are a single dialog with Escape/close and focus return; `prefers-reduced-motion` disables momentum and animated jumps (auto-scroll stays available on explicit request).
- **OG images** are generated once with Playwright's Chromium and committed, so the Pages build needs no browser.
- **Imprint/privacy** are placeholders to be completed by the operator (§ 5 DDG, § 18 MStV, GDPR controller data).
